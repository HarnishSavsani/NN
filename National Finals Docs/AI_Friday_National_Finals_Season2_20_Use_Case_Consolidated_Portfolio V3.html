Business Context & Why It Matters

Why this problem matters

Disputes contain conflicting customer, merchant and system narratives, and important facts can arrive late after the case has already progressed.

The operating environment spans customer statements, merchant records, transaction events, receipts, delivery records, authentication events and correspondence. Evidence can arrive asynchronously, be corrected later or conflict across systems, so the solution must preserve state, provenance and prior actions as the case evolves.

Complexity layer: The solution must reconcile changing evidence across multiple systems, maintain long-running state, support real approvals and continue safely through partial failures. Material changes must trigger targeted re-evaluation rather than silently preserving an outdated conclusion.
02

The Challenge & Scope

Problem Statement 01 ·

Cards & Payments · Payments Operations · Evidence Intelligence

What teams must reimagine

Reconstruct a trustworthy disputed event, expose gaps and contradictions, and recommend the next useful evidence or permitted case action.

The outcome should be an evidence-backed operational workspace that can progress the journey, surface uncertainty and coordinate authorized actions while preserving accountability. Final liability and consequential case decisions remain human-owned.

03

Core Solution Capabilities

Your solution should be able to

  • Assemble and reconcile customer statements, merchant records, transaction events, receipts, delivery records, authentication events and correspondence into a coherent, time-aware operating view.
  • Detect missing, stale, duplicated or contradictory information and make uncertainty explicit.
  • Dynamically select the next useful evidence, analysis, coordination or permitted action based on the current state and constraints.
  • Maintain source provenance, human intervention, approvals and a complete audit trail through the full journey.
04

End-to-End Capability Expectations & Controls

End-to-End Capability Expectations

The following capabilities are part of the main challenge scope and should be addressed in the solution.

  • Build a durable domain model that links customer statements, merchant records, transaction events, receipts, delivery records, authentication events and correspondence and can represent late, corrected or conflicting versions without losing earlier evidence.
  • Determine the next-best evidence request or permitted action dynamically from current state, deadlines, dependencies, authority and expected value rather than relying on a fixed happy path.
  • Track long-running workflow state, deadlines and cross-system dependencies while preventing duplicate requests, duplicate transactions or repeated external actions.
  • Represent competing interpretations, plans or hypotheses and show which evidence supports, weakens or changes each option without hiding uncertainty.
  • Maintain versioned state with source-level provenance and explicit separation of recorded fact, AI inference, user input, automated action and human decision.
  • Coordinate approved tools or specialist AI components with permission checks, retries, timeouts, idempotency and safe recovery when only part of a workflow succeeds.
  • Support role-based intervention, approval gates, audit replay and configurable action boundaries so reviewers can reconstruct what the system knew, why it acted and what changed afterward.

Business Context & Why It Matters

Why this problem matters

Supply chains operate through interconnected planning and operational processes spanning suppliers, plants, warehouses, transportation and customers. Disruptions can invalidate an approved plan quickly, while local fixes can create downstream shortages, service failures or excess cost.

The challenge is to build an intelligent decision platform that understands the current operating state, identifies material exceptions, evaluates their downstream impact and recommends feasible recovery actions before execution changes are committed.

Complexity layer: The solution must reconcile changing operational evidence, constraints and dependencies across a multi-node network, maintain state over a long-running recovery workflow, preserve provenance and approvals, and recover safely from partial execution or changed conditions.
02

The Challenge & Scope

What teams must reimagine

Build a production-ready AI solution that starts from a feasible baseline plan and continuously monitors execution, detects disruption, investigates affected dependencies, generates alternative responses, evaluates those alternatives through simulation and recommends the best recovery path.

The solution should balance service, cost, inventory, capacity and business priorities while keeping planner approval as the final control for material plan changes.

03

Core Solution Capabilities

Your solution should be able to

  • Ingest baseline demand / forecast signals, sales and orders, supplier commitments, capacity calendars, inventory, transport lanes and operational status.
  • Maintain a time-aware representation of plants, suppliers, warehouses, customers and transport dependencies.
  • Detect structured and unstructured disruptions and identify affected products, orders, facilities, lanes and customer commitments.
  • Explain the causal chain from the disruption to operational and business impact.
  • Generate feasible recovery alternatives such as alternate suppliers, rerouting, production reallocation, inventory redeployment, prioritisation and expediting.
  • Simulate the operational consequences of alternatives against constraints and selected business objectives.
  • Recommend a recovery option with clear evidence, trade-offs, assumptions and confidence.
  • Support planner approval, revised-plan generation, execution tracking and an auditable record of what changed.
04

End-to-End Capability Expectations & Controls

End-to-End Capability Expectations

  • Operational state model: Represent the current and planned state of suppliers, plants, warehouses, inventory, orders, transport and customer commitments, including late or corrected information.
  • Exception investigation: Connect events across the network and distinguish root causes from secondary symptoms.
  • Constraint-aware recovery: Ensure proposed actions respect capacity, supply, transport, inventory and service constraints.
  • Scenario generation: Create multiple editable response options rather than a single hard-coded recovery.
  • Simulation-backed evaluation: Compare service, cost, inventory, utilisation, timing and other selected objectives before commitment.
  • Dynamic prioritisation: Re-evaluate recommendations when material facts, constraints or priorities change.
  • Safe orchestration: Enforce permissions, approvals, idempotency, retries, timeouts and partial-failure recovery for external or simulated actions.
  • Traceability: Keep source provenance and distinguish recorded facts, AI inferences, user decisions, simulated outcomes and committed actions.

The solution should demonstrate network-state understanding, disruption detection, investigation, alternative generation, simulation, recommendation, human approval and revised-plan execution. Representative mock interfaces or simulated services are permitted where live enterprise systems are unavailable, but security, resilience, observability, auditability and maintainability remain part of the expectation.

Data Availability & Synthetic Data

Teams may use synthetic, open or self-generated data. The demonstration should contain a realistic multi-product network with suppliers, plants, warehouses, customers and transport links, plus baseline plans and deliberately introduced disruptions. Include structured operational data and representative communications such as emails or exception notices. No live enterprise data or live supply-chain platform access is required.

Business Context & Why It Matters

Why this problem matters

Procurement savings are often constrained before negotiation starts. Requirements may contain excess quantities or unnecessarily premium specifications, category strategies may rely on incomplete market context, supplier bids may use different units and pricing structures, and negotiated value can be weakened when translated into contract language.

The opportunity is to build a connected AI solution that challenges demand, improves sourcing and price outcomes, and protects the negotiated value until contract signature.

02

The Challenge & Scope

Problem Statement 03 · Procurement ·

Procurement · Source-to-Contract · Commercial Decision Intelligence

What teams must reimagine

Build a production-ready AI solution that starts with an initial procurement requirement and ends with a signed-contract-ready commercial position. The solution should reason across requirements, consumption and spend data, market signals, known supplier bids, negotiation positions and contract documents.

Every material recommendation must be traceable to evidence and high-impact or ambiguous decisions must be routed for human review.

1. Challenge & Optimise the Demand

  • Test requested volumes against historical consumption, forecasts and actual need.
  • Identify duplicate, fragmented or overlapping requests that can be consolidated.
  • Assess whether quantities can be reduced, phased or timed differently.
  • Challenge unnecessary buffers, minimums or contingency volumes.
  • Simplify materials, features, brands, technical requirements or service levels where appropriate.
  • Assess substitutes, alternative designs or equivalent offerings.
  • Identify specifications that unnecessarily restrict competition or raise cost.
  • Surface supplier-led innovation, alternative commercial models and lifecycle-cost improvements.

2. Get the Price Right

  • Use spend and market signals to determine a defensible category approach.
  • Develop an independent should-cost or fair-price position using relevant cost drivers, volumes, benchmarks and transparent assumptions.
  • Explain why the commercial position is economically defensible.
  • Assume a known supplier pool and orchestrate an RFx around the optimised requirement.
  • Normalise bids across currencies, units, bundles, tiers and commercial terms.
  • Identify hidden costs and conditions and evaluate total value rather than headline price.
  • Establish negotiation targets, levers, trade-offs, fallbacks and walk-away positions.
  • Recommend a negotiation position or controlled next action; bounded automation may be demonstrated within clear guardrails.

Synthetic Supplier Bids

Teams should create or use deliberately non-normalised supplier bids with varied currencies, units, bundles, tiers, hidden costs and commercial conditions. The solution must perform the normalisation and total-value reasoning rather than receive a pre-cleaned comparison.

3. Lock the Value into a Tight Contract

  • Translate negotiated commercial positions into appropriate contract clauses and schedules.
  • Draft tight, standards-aligned language without inventing unsupported obligations or terms.
  • Compare supplier redlines with the agreed commercial position.
  • Explain the savings or value implication of material redlines.
  • Identify language that weakens pricing, rebates, service levels, commitments or protections.
  • Route ambiguous or high-impact changes for human review.
03

Core Solution Capabilities

Connected Requirement-to-Contract View

The solution should maintain one traceable thread from the original requirement and category strategy through supplier bids, negotiated position and contract clauses.

Evidence chain: Requirement → Optimised Need → Category Strategy → Should-Cost → RFx/Bids → Negotiated Position → Contract Language → Value Assurance

State, Evidence & Provenance

Maintain versioned operating state with traceable source evidence, timestamps and explicit separation between recorded facts, AI inference, proposed actions, approved actions and verified outcomes.

Dynamic Reassessment

When material evidence changes, re-evaluate only the affected assumptions, dependencies, risks and actions; preserve valid completed work and explain what changed and why.

Human Decision Boundaries

Keep consequential business, commercial, safety, regulatory or customer-impacting decisions behind explicit role-based approval gates and configurable action boundaries.

Reliable Orchestration

Coordinate permitted workflows with preconditions, permissions, checkpoints, retries, timeouts, idempotency, partial-failure handling, human takeover and safe resume.

Contradiction & Uncertainty Control

Surface missing, stale, duplicated or contradictory information, retain competing interpretations where required and avoid unsupported conclusions when evidence is insufficient.

Audit & Outcome Validation

Provide an inspectable replay of evidence, reasoning, approvals and actions, and independently validate the resulting business or operational outcome before closure.

Business Context & Why It Matters

03 Commercial Banking · Credit Risk

Build an AI-driven early-warning capability that continuously evaluates borrower financials, account activity, payment behaviour, utilization, covenant thresholds, industry deterioration, news, concentration exposure and treasury flows to predict potential covenant breaches 30, 60 and 90 days in advance.

/90-Day PredictionFinancial ReasoningRisk SignalsExplainable Intervention

Business Context

Commercial lending covenants are often monitored through periodic borrower reporting and manual review. A borrower can deteriorate between reporting cycles, while warning signals may already exist across account activity, utilization, payments, treasury flows, industry conditions and concentration exposure.

Earlier detection can help relationship, credit and risk teams intervene before a formal breach occurs.

02

The Challenge & Scope

Problem Statement

Design and build a production-ready AI solution that monitors contractual covenant thresholds together with borrower financial and behavioural signals, forecasts breach risk over 30-, 60- and 90-day horizons, explains the drivers of deterioration, and recommends prioritized interventions.

Core challenge: The system must distinguish meaningful deterioration from temporary noise and provide evidence-backed early warning rather than merely flagging a covenant after it has already been breached.
03

Core Solution Capabilities

Required Solution Capabilities

  • Ingest borrower financial statements and calculate relevant financial ratios.
  • Extract and represent covenant definitions, thresholds, testing frequency and exceptions.
  • Monitor account activity, payment behaviour and credit/facility utilization.
  • Evaluate treasury flows and changes in cash movement patterns.
  • Incorporate concentration exposure and synthetic industry/news deterioration signals.
  • Predict probability of covenant breach at 30-, 60- and 90-day horizons.
  • Identify the primary drivers contributing to forecast risk.
  • Rank borrowers/facilities by urgency and expected impact.
  • Recommend appropriate relationship-manager, credit or risk-team interventions.
  • Generate an auditable warning trail showing data, trends, calculations and reasoning.

Early-Warning Intelligence

The solution should combine deterministic covenant calculations with predictive indicators. Examples include:

  • Weakening debt-service or leverage position
  • Rapid utilization increase
  • Delayed or irregular payments
  • Deteriorating cash inflows
  • Industry stress
  • Concentration risk
  • Unusual treasury-flow changes
04

End-to-End Capability Expectations & Controls

Covenant Definition & Calculation Control

Represent each covenant with its definition, threshold, testing frequency and applicable exceptions, and calculate the relevant financial ratios consistently from the borrower financial data. The solution should keep the contractual covenant condition distinct from behavioural or external early-warning indicators.

Time-Aware Signal Monitoring

Continuously reconcile borrower financials with account activity, payment behaviour, facility utilization, treasury flows, concentration exposure and industry or news deterioration signals. Changes in these signals should update the borrower risk view without losing the underlying evidence or historical trend.

/ 90-Day Risk Horizon & Driver Traceability

Produce breach-risk assessments across 30-, 60- and 90-day horizons and clearly identify the primary drivers behind each warning. Every warning should show the covenant movement, supporting behavioural or external signals, the relevant trend and the evidence used to reach the conclusion.

False-Positive & Materiality Control

Distinguish sustained deterioration from temporary noise before escalating a borrower. The solution should make confidence and materiality visible, avoid unnecessary escalation where the evidence is weak or transient, and allow the risk view to be revised when later evidence changes the interpretation.

Portfolio Prioritization & Intervention Governance

Rank borrowers or facilities by urgency, confidence and potential exposure, then recommend appropriate relationship-manager, credit or risk-team interventions. Recommended actions should remain advisory and should support human review for material credit, covenant-waiver or escalation decisions.

Auditability & Explainable Early Warning

Maintain an auditable warning history showing source data, covenant calculations, trend changes, risk assessments, driver explanations, prioritization and recommended interventions so reviewers can reconstruct why a warning was raised and how the view changed over time.

Control emphasis: Covenant accuracy, advance warning, false-positive control, driver explainability, portfolio prioritization, actionability and complete audit history should remain visible throughout the workflow.
05

Production-Ready Solution & Data Environment

Development Environment

Teams should create synthetic commercial borrower portfolios with financials, covenants, account activity, payment history, facility utilization, industry indicators and treasury flows.

  • No live core-banking or credit system integration is required.
  • External news/industry signals may be supplied or synthetically generated.
  • The full scoring and alerting workflow should be executable locally.
